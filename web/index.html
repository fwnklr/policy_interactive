How it works

Each model is summarized by its responses to anticipated ("news") monetary policy shocks in every future quarter. Stacked together, these impulse responses map any path of policy shocks into paths for the economy: y = ȳ + M x, where ȳ is the SEP baseline. A policy rule or the first-order conditions of optimal control under commitment pin down the shocks x that implement that policy against the baseline. The effective lower bound turns this into a linear complementarity problem, solved exactly. The counterfactual starts in the quarter of the SEP release; earlier quarters are data.

Models: a linearized version of the Federal Reserve Board's FRB/US model (LINVER), and the Del Negro–Giannoni–Schorfheide model with finite-horizon planning (DGS-FHP). Baselines: FOMC Summary of Economic Projections, extended beyond the SEP horizon.
