How it works

Each model's response to monetary policy is summarized by its sequence-space representation, and every counterfactual here — a simple rule or a fully optimal path under commitment, with or without the effective lower bound — is solved using the efficient method of Hebden and Winkler.1 The counterfactual starts in the quarter of the SEP release; earlier quarters are historical data.

Three models are available. The first is a linearized version of the Federal Reserve's FRB/US model (LINVER),2 a large-scale estimated general-equilibrium model of the U.S. economy in use at the Federal Reserve Board since 1996. The second is the DGS-FHP model,3 a modified version of the model of del Negro, Giannoni and Schorfheide4 that attenuates the forward-guidance puzzle through finite-horizon planning for households; it is more sensitive to interest rates than FRB/US. The third is the Smets–Wouters (2007) model,5 a standard estimated New Keynesian model of the U.S. economy; in this model, unemployment is obtained from the output gap through an Okun's law relation (u − u* = −0.5 × gap). Each baseline is an SEP-consistent projection6 — the quarterly projection consistent with that quarter's FOMC Summary of Economic Projections — extended beyond the SEP horizon; GDP growth is quarterly and annualized.

Economic projections are collected from each member of the Board of Governors and each Federal Reserve Bank president four times a year, in connection with the FOMC meetings in March, June, September and December. Participants provide yearly projections for the current and next two or three calendar years, and for the “longer run,” of real GDP growth, the unemployment rate, and headline and core PCE inflation, along with their individual assumptions for the appropriate federal funds rate. Federal Reserve staff use a model-guided interpolation and extrapolation procedure, together with current economic data, to build quarterly series consistent with these projections; the resulting series need not represent the projections of the Committee or of any participant. The staff regularly publishes these series as part of the FRB/US model package, available at federalreserve.gov/econres/us-models-package.htm. ↩
